## 💰 VALUATION MATH (Protect Your Equity)

### **How Valuations Work**

**Formula:**
Post-Money Valuation = Investment Amount / Equity %

**Example:**
- Investor gives: $1M
- You give up: 20% equity
- Post-money valuation: $1M / 0.20 = $5M
- Pre-money valuation: $5M - $1M = $4M

**Your goal:** Maximize valuation (minimize equity given up)

## ❌ RED FLAG TERMS (DON'T ACCEPT)

### **Red Flag #1: Full Ratchet Anti-Dilution**

**What it means:**
If you raise Series A at lower valuation, investor's shares increase automatically (they're protected, you're diluted).

**Example:**
- Seed: Investor bought 20% at $5M valuation
- Series A: You raise at $4M valuation
- With full ratchet: Investor's 20% increases to 25% (you're diluted extra)

**Protection:** Weighted average anti-dilution (standard)

**Red flag language:**
"Full ratchet anti-dilution clause"

---

### **Red Flag #2: High Liquidation Preference**

**What it means:**
If the company sells for less than the valuation, investor gets paid first. Multiple times.

**Example:**
- You raise $1M at $5M valuation
- Company sells for $3M
- With 2x liquidation preference: Investor gets $2M (double)
- Founders get: $1M (leftover)
- Result: Founders lose money on "successful" exit

**Protection:** 1x liquidation preference (standard)

**Red flag language:**
"2x or 3x liquidation preference"

---

### **Red Flag #3: Investor Control**

**What it means:**
Investor gets veto rights on major decisions.

**Examples of bad control terms:**
- "Investor must approve all hiring >$50K"
- "Investor must approve all new product features"
- "Investor can remove CEO without cause"
- "Investor has majority board seats"

**Protection:** Advisor-only (non-voting)

**Red flag language:**
"Investor approval required for..."

---

### **Red Flag #4: Exclusivity**

**What it means:**
You can only raise from this investor. Can't talk to others.

**Why it's bad:**
- Kills leverage (they know no one else will fund)
- Delays your raise (they control timeline)
- Increases chance they demand bad terms

**Protection:** Never accept exclusivity

**Red flag language:**
"Exclusive negotiation period of X months"

---

### **Red Flag #5: Forced Pivot**

**What it means:**
Investor says "I'll fund if you change your product"

**Example:**
"Streaming won't work. Build crypto instead, then I'll invest."

**Why it's bad:**
- Kills your vision
- They don't believe in YOU
- Sets pattern for more demands

**Protection:** Walk away

**Red flag language:**
"I love your team, but the product needs to..."

---

### **Red Flag #6: Unreasonable MCC (Minimum Cash Close)**

**What it means:**
Investor will only invest if you hit unrealistic metrics first.

**Example:**
"I'll fund when you hit 10K creators"

**Why it's bad:**
- Circular (you need capital TO hit metrics)
- Moves goalpost (they keep increasing requirements)

**Protection:** MCC should be <25% harder than projected

**Red flag language:**
"We'll fund when you hit [impossible metric]"

---

### **Red Flag #7: Personal Guarantees**

**What it means:**
If company fails, you personally owe the debt.

**Why it's bad:**
- Unlimited personal liability
- They can come after your house

**Protection:** Never sign personal guarantees

**Red flag language:**
"We'll need your personal guarantee on this"

---

### **Red Flag #8: Information Rights (Overreach)**

**What it means:**
Investor gets access to ALL company data, forever.

**Examples of overreach:**
- "Quarterly access to all financial data" ✅ OK
- "Monthly access to all employee information" ❌ Excessive
- "Veto rights on hiring/firing" ❌ Excessive
- "Access to all customer data/analytics" ❌ Excessive

**Protection:** Standard reporting (quarterly financials only)

---

### **Red Flag #9: Participation Rights**

**What it means:**
Investor automatically gets to invest in all future rounds (dilutes other investors).

**Why it's bad:**
- Limits who else can invest
- Creates drama if investor doesn't have capital

**Protection:** Pro-rata rights only (if you want more, pay pro-rata)

---

### **Red Flag #10: No Side Letter / Amendment Rights**

**What it means:**
Once you sign, terms are locked. Can't change anything.

**Why it's bad:**
- Circumstances change
- You're stuck with bad terms forever

**Protection:** Allow for amendment by mutual consent

## 📋 THE TERM SHEET (What You'll See)

**A term sheet is the investor's proposed contract.**

### **Key Sections:**

1. **Investment Amount**
   - How much they invest: $500K, $1M, etc.

2. **Valuation**
   - Pre-money and post-money valuations
   - This determines how much equity they get

3. **Equity**
   - % of company investor receives
   - Your ownership after deal

4. **Liquidation Preference**
   - How payouts work in exit/acquisition
   - 1x (standard) or 2x+ (red flag)

5. **Anti-Dilution**
   - Weighted average (good) or full ratchet (bad)
   - Protects investor from future dilution

6. **Board Seats**
   - Investor usually gets 1 board seat
   - You should have majority

7. **Information Rights**
   - What data investor gets
   - Usually: quarterly financials

8. **Protective Provisions**
   - What major decisions need investor approval
   - Should be LIMITED

9. **Registration Rights**
   - For IPO (not relevant for seed stage)
   - Can ignore

10. **Closing Conditions**
    - What needs to happen before money transfers
    - Usually: legal review, background check, etc.

## 🎯 NEGOTIATION STRATEGY

### **Phase 1: Understand Their Position**

Before negotiating, know:
- [ ] How much capital do they have? (check their recent deals)
- [ ] Are they desperate to deploy? (fast-moving VCs are more flexible)
- [ ] Do they have FOMO? (other investors interested = leverage)
- [ ] What's their thesis? (align on mission)
- [ ] Who else have they funded? (understand their pattern)

---

### **Phase 2: Set Your Anchors**

**Anchor #1: Valuation**
"Based on our metrics, I value the company at $10M pre-money. At that valuation, $1M is 9% equity. That's the range I'm looking at."

**Why:** First number anchors negotiation

---

### **Phase 3: Identify Deal-Breakers**

**Your non-negotiables:**
- "I won't give up >20% in seed round"
- "No investor control over product decisions"
- "No personal guarantees"
- "Weighted average anti-dilution only"

**Communicate these early.** Saves time.

---

### **Phase 4: Negotiate One Term at a Time**

**DON'T:**
Investor: "Here's our term sheet" (20 bad terms)
You: Accept all at once

**DO:**
You: "Let's go through each term. Start with valuation."
Investor: "$3M pre-money"
You: "I'm at $8M. Let's work toward $6M."
[Agree on valuation]
Next term: "Now anti-dilution..."

**One term at a time.** Takes longer but you win more.

---

### **Phase 5: Trade Terms**

**You won't win on everything.**

**Example trades:**
- "You get board observer seat IF I keep 90%+ founder equity" ✅
- "You get quarterly reporting IF liquidation pref stays 1x" ✅
- "You get pro-rata rights IF anti-dilution is weighted average" ✅

**The goal:** Get what matters most to you.

---

### **Phase 6: Get Outside Legal Review**

**NEVER sign without a lawyer.**

**Cost:** $2K-$5K (worth it)
**Find:** Search "startup lawyer near me" or ask other founders

**Lawyer will catch:**
- Hidden penalties
- Bad phrasing
- Problematic provisions
- Unfair terms

---

### **Phase 7: Final Negotiation**

Once terms are mostly agreed:

"Here's what I'm hearing. Let me confirm:
- Valuation: $6M pre-money ✅
- Equity: 14% ✅
- Anti-dilution: Weighted average ✅
- Liquidation: 1x non-participating ✅
- Board: Observer only ✅

Am I missing anything?"

[Confirm alignment before signing]

## 🚨 NEGOTIATION RED FLAGS (From Investor)

### **Red Flag Language #1: "Sign this to move forward"**

Translation: "I'm pressuring you"
Response: "I always get legal review. This is standard founder practice."

---

### **Red Flag Language #2: "If you don't close by X date, I'm out"**

Translation: "I'm creating artificial urgency"
Response: "I understand your timeline. Let's work together to close faster, but I won't compromise terms."

---

### **Red Flag Language #3: "Other investors got [worse terms]"**

Translation: "I'm using guilt"
Response: "I'm sure they did. Those investors made different trade-offs. I value [X], so I'm willing to accept [Y]."

---

### **Red Flag Language #4: "Trust me, this is standard"**

Translation: "I'm bullshitting"
Response: "I believe you. Let me get legal review to confirm."

---

### **Red Flag Language #5: "If you're not comfortable, I can find other founders"**

Translation: "I'm threatening to leave"
Response: "I appreciate that. I'm also talking to other investors. Let's see if this is a fit for both of us."

## 💬 PHRASES TO USE IN NEGOTIATION

### **Assertive but Not Aggressive:**

✅ "I appreciate the offer. Let me share why I think [X is better]..."
✅ "That works for me IF we also get [Y]..."
✅ "I need to check with my advisor on that..."
✅ "Walk me through your thinking on [term]..."
✅ "Is that a hard line for you or room to negotiate?"
✅ "What if we did [compromise]?"
✅ "I'm excited about this partnership. Let's find terms we both love."

### **Don't Say:**

❌ "We need this" (sounds desperate)
❌ "That's not fair" (sounds emotional)
❌ "Other investors offered [X]" (sounds like you're shopping)
❌ "I'll walk" (threats kill deals)
❌ "You're screwing me" (aggressive, kills partnership)
