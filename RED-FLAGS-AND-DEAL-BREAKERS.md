# NYXSCREAM - RED FLAGS & DEAL BREAKERS

**The most important skill: Knowing when to say NO**
**A bad investor is worse than no investor**

---

## 🚨 INVESTOR PERSONALITY RED FLAGS

### **Red Flag: The Bully**

**What it looks like:**
- Aggressive language ("This is non-negotiable")
- Rushing decisions ("Sign by Friday or I'm out")
- Taking credit for ideas
- Dismissive of your concerns
- Name-dropping to intimidate

**Examples:**
- "I've funded 50 startups. Trust me, this is how it works."
- "If you don't like it, I can find 10 other founders."
- "Stop negotiating. Just sign."

**Why it's bad:**
- Sets tone for partnership (they'll bully you later)
- Future disagreements will be one-sided
- They won't help when you need it

**Your response:**
"I appreciate the offer, but I need a partnership where we both respect each other. If this feels adversarial now, I'm concerned about future interactions."

[Walk away]

---

### **Red Flag: The Micromanager**

**What it looks like:**
- Asking for daily updates
- Wanting approval on hiring
- Questioning every product decision
- Wanting real-time access to all systems
- Treating you like an employee

**Examples:**
- "I need daily standup calls"
- "Show me every line of code before deployment"
- "I don't approve of hiring [person]"
- "Let me sit in on customer calls"

**Why it's bad:**
- Kills founder autonomy
- Slows decision-making
- Creates toxic relationship
- Prevents you from taking risks

**Your response:**
"I appreciate your engagement. Here's how I'd like to structure communication: [monthly calls, quarterly reports, etc.]. That way you're informed but I can move fast."

[If they won't agree, walk]

---

### **Red Flag: The Flake**

**What it looks like:**
- Slow to respond to emails
- Cancels meetings repeatedly
- Says they'll help but doesn't follow through
- Doesn't attend board meetings
- Disappears when you need them

**Examples:**
- Takes 2 weeks to respond to urgent email
- Cancels 3 investor calls in a row
- "I'll introduce you to [contact]" (never does)
- Promises to help with fundraising (doesn't)

**Why it's bad:**
- Capital alone doesn't help
- You need active partnership
- When you're in trouble, they won't be there
- Sends signal to other investors (other founders will say they're unreliable)

**Your response:**
"Before we finalize, I want to confirm communication expectations. How often can I reach you if there's an urgent issue?"

[Listen to their answer. If vague, walk]

---

### **Red Flag: The Ideologue**

**What it looks like:**
- "You MUST do it this way"
- Imposing their vision on your product
- Won't listen to market feedback
- "I know what works. Trust me."
- Takes credit if it works, blames you if it doesn't

**Examples:**
- "Streaming is dead. Pivot to Web3."
- "Your pricing is wrong. It should be $99/month."
- "Fire your CTO, hire someone I know."

**Why it's bad:**
- They're investing in THEIR idea, not your idea
- You lose autonomy and credibility with team
- If they're wrong, you both lose
- Creates founder resentment

**Your response:**
"I appreciate the input. But this is my company and my vision. I need an investor who supports MY direction, even if they disagree sometimes."

[Walk]

---

### **Red Flag: The Drama Queen**

**What it looks like:**
- Emotional, unpredictable reactions
- Takes disagreements personally
- Shares your struggles publicly
- Gossiping about other companies they funded
- Mood swings in conversations

**Examples:**
- "After all I've done for you, you won't listen to me?"
- Mentions confidential metrics in public
- Tells other founders bad things about you

**Why it's bad:**
- Unpredictable partnership
- Your confidential info isn't safe
- Damages your reputation
- Creates stress that affects decision-making

**Your response:**
"I want a professional relationship. If that's not possible, let's walk."

[Walk]

---

### **Red Flag: The Predator**

**What it looks like:**
- Inappropriate personal comments
- Boundary violations
- Power-play behavior
- Isolation tactics (wants meetings alone)
- Unwanted physical contact

**Examples:**
- Commenting on appearance
- "Dinner at my place to discuss business"
- Suggesting personal relationship
- Touching you without permission

**Why it's bad:**
- Sexual harassment (illegal)
- Abuse of power
- Your safety is at risk

**Your response:**
"This is inappropriate. I'm not interested in this dynamic. Let's end this conversation."

[Walk immediately. Don't look back. Tell your lawyer if it escalates.]

## 💼 BUSINESS RED FLAGS (Terms & Behavior)

### **Red Flag: Weird Valuation Math**

**What it looks like:**
- Valuation doesn't match market comparables
- They won't explain how they got to the number
- Valuation depends on unrealistic metrics
- Different valuation for different founders

**Examples:**
- "$1M valuation" (way too low for production-ready app)
- "I'll value you at $10M IF you hit 100K creators first" (circular logic)
- Valuing similar companies at $5M but yours at $2M (without reason)

**Red flag language:**
"I value companies based on my gut feel"
"Your product is interesting but risky" (without specifics)
"Let's start low and increase next round"

**Why it's bad:**
- Undervaluation dilutes you unnecessarily
- Sets bad precedent for future rounds
- Shows they don't understand your market

**Your response:**
"Can you walk me through your valuation methodology? I want to understand your thinking."

[If they can't explain it, walk]

---

### **Red Flag: Unrealistic MCC (Minimum Closing Conditions)**

**What it looks like:**
- MCC requires you to hit metrics BEFORE they fund
- MCC is harder than your projections
- MCC is out of your control
- MCC keeps moving

**Examples:**
- "Fund when you hit 50K creators" (but you need capital to recruit)
- "Fund when revenue is $1M" (circular: you need capital to grow revenue)
- "Prove profitability first" (circular: you need capital to optimize)

**Why it's bad:**
- You can't hit metrics without capital
- Investor controls timeline
- Goalpost keeps moving

**Your response:**
"MCC should be 25% harder than projections, not 300% harder. What's realistic?"

[If they won't budge, walk]

---

### **Red Flag: Exclusivity Clause**

**What it looks like:**
- "You can't talk to other investors"
- "You can't fundraise from competitors of my portfolio"
- "If you talk to [investor], deal is off"

**Red flag language:**
"Exclusive negotiation period of 90 days"
"You need to choose: us or them"

**Why it's bad:**
- Kills your leverage
- Gives investor all the power
- They know you have no backup plan
- Delays funding (they won't rush)

**Your response:**
"I'm not comfortable with exclusivity. I'm raising from multiple investors simultaneously. That's standard practice."

[Walk if they insist]

---

### **Red Flag: Strange Timeline**

**What it looks like:**
- "We'll fund in 6 months" (too long for urgency)
- "You need to decide by tomorrow" (artificial urgency)
- Keep pushing closing date back
- "We have to wait for [committee/partner] to approve" (no clear timeline)

**Why it's bad:**
- Too long = you'll find capital elsewhere anyway
- Too short = they're pressuring you
- Constantly moving = not committed
- Waiting for approval = not decision-maker

**Your response:**
"What's your realistic timeline? I need to understand your process."

[If vague, they're not serious]

---

### **Red Flag: Weird Legal Requests**

**What it looks like:**
- Asking for personal guarantee
- Asking you to sign NDA (before they pitch you)
- Asking for cap table (before they commit)
- Asking for equity before investment
- Wanting your personal home as collateral

**Red flag language:**
"Just a standard personal guarantee"
"We need your home as collateral"
"Sign NDA so we can discuss"

**Why it's bad:**
- Personal guarantee = unlimited personal liability
- Home collateral = you lose your house if company fails
- NDA before pitch = sketchy
- Asking for equity = they want free options

**Your response:**
"I don't sign personal guarantees. That's not standard for venture funding."

[Walk]

---

### **Red Flag: No Written Term Sheet**

**What it looks like:**
- Investor keeps saying "We're on the same page" but never sends written terms
- Keeps verbal commitments, no paper
- Months go by without written agreement
- When finally documented, terms are different

**Why it's bad:**
- No clarity on actual deal
- Investor can change story later ("I never said that")
- You have no legal protection
- Creates confusion with team

**Your response:**
"I'd like to get everything in writing. Can you send a term sheet this week?"

[If they refuse, walk. Serious investors put it in writing.]

---

### **Red Flag: Multiple Investment Rounds Disguised as Seed**

**What it looks like:**
- "I'll invest $250K now, then $500K later"
- "First close is $100K, second close is $900K"
- They keep wanting to add new conditions for future tranches

**Why it's bad:**
- Leverage to change terms later ("Or we won't fund the second close")
- You think you have $1M but only have $250K
- Creates uncertainty

**Your response:**
"I need all $1M committed upfront. I can't build on partial capital commitments."

[Walk if they won't commit]

## 🚫 ABSOLUTE DEAL BREAKERS (Walk Immediately)

### **Deal Breaker #1: Personal Guarantee**

**They're asking:** Your personal liability if company fails

**Your response:** NO

**Why:** You're risking your house. Venture investors NEVER require this.

---

### **Deal Breaker #2: Founder Removal Rights**

**They're asking:** Ability to fire you without cause

**Your response:** NO

**Why:** This is your company. You should only be fired for cause.

---

### **Deal Breaker #3: Forced Pivot**

**They're asking:** Change your product/business model

**Your response:** NO

**Why:** They're investing in YOU and YOUR vision, not THEIR vision.

---

### **Deal Breaker #4: Sexual Harassment / Inappropriate Behavior**

**They're doing:** Any unwanted sexual comments, advances, or behavior

**Your response:** END IT IMMEDIATELY

**Why:** This is abuse. You don't need their money. Ever.

---

### **Deal Breaker #5: Full Ratchet Anti-Dilution**

**They're asking:** Automatic share increase if future round is lower valuation

**Your response:** NO

**Why:** This is punitive to founders. Weighted average is fair.

---

### **Deal Breaker #6: Liquidation Preference >1x**

**They're asking:** 2x, 3x, or higher liquidation preference

**Your response:** NO

**Why:** At acquisition, investor gets paid 2-3x before you see anything.

---

### **Deal Breaker #7: Majority Control**

**They're asking:** Investor has veto on major decisions

**Your response:** NO

**Why:** You lose autonomy. They can block everything.

---

### **Deal Breaker #8: Exclusivity**

**They're asking:** You can only raise from them

**Your response:** NO

**Why:** Kills your leverage. They'll never close.

---

### **Deal Breaker #9: Unreasonable MCC**

**They're asking:** Hit impossible metrics BEFORE they fund

**Your response:** NO

**Why:** You can't hit metrics without capital. It's circular.

---

### **Deal Breaker #10: Misaligned Values**

**They're asking:** Compromise your ethics for profit

**Your response:** NO

**Why:** Your mental health > their money. You'll regret it.

## ✅ INVESTOR GREEN FLAGS (Worth Saying YES)

### **Green Flag #1: They've Done This Before**

**What it looks like:**
- Funded 10+ companies in your space
- Several exits (acquisition or IPO)
- Portfolio companies are doing well
- Founders speak highly of them

**Why it's good:**
- They understand the space
- They know how to help
- They won't panic if things are slow
- Their network is valuable

---

### **Green Flag #2: They Listen More Than Talk**

**What it looks like:**
- Ask questions about your vision
- Try to understand your market
- Offer suggestions, not demands
- Respect your decision-making

**Examples:**
"Tell me more about why you chose this approach"
"What are you worried about?"
"How can I help without getting in your way?"

**Why it's good:**
- They'll be supportive partner
- They respect founder autonomy
- They'll help you think, not just command

---

### **Green Flag #3: They Lead With Value, Not Terms**

**What it looks like:**
- "Here's how I can help beyond capital: [specific value]"
- Introduce you to 5+ relevant people
- Offer expertise in specific areas
- Connect you with customers

**Examples:**
"I know the head of content at [Company]. Want an intro?"
"I've built this product before. Happy to pair on strategy."
"Here's how I'd approach your go-to-market."

**Why it's good:**
- Capital is table stakes. Value is rare.
- They're invested in your success
- You'll learn from them

---

### **Green Flag #4: They're Transparent About Terms**

**What it looks like:**
- "Here's our standard term sheet"
- "Here's what we usually negotiate on"
- "What matters most to you?"
- "Let's find something that works for both of us"

**Why it's good:**
- No hidden agenda
- Reasonable expectations
- Partnership mindset

---

### **Green Flag #5: They Have Skin in the Game**

**What it looks like:**
- They've invested their own money (not just managing others' capital)
- They sit on your board
- They're accessible (not just capital provider)
- They care about your success

**Why it's good:**
- Aligned incentives
- They'll help when times are tough
- More patient with growth

---

### **Green Flag #6: They Respect Your Timeline**

**What it looks like:**
- "When do you need capital?"
- "Let's work at your pace"
- No artificial urgency
- Flexible closing date

**Why it's good:**
- They're not pressuring you
- You can negotiate fairly
- They're focused on fit, not speed

---

### **Green Flag #7: They Know Your Market**

**What it looks like:**
- They've funded competitors (or adjacent companies)
- They understand your TAM
- They ask informed questions
- They know your challenges

**Why it's good:**
- They can advise from experience
- They won't waste your time
- They understand what works

---

### **Green Flag #8: They're Mission-Aligned**

**What it looks like:**
- They believe in your vision (not just the profit)
- They ask about impact, not just revenue
- They want to support creators
- They respect your values

**Why it's good:**
- Partnership will be fulfilling
- They won't push you to compromise
- Shared purpose = better relationship

---

### **Green Flag #9: They Move Fast (But Fairly)**

**What it looks like:**
- Can turn around term sheet in 1 week
- Responsive to emails (within 24 hours)
- Make decisions quickly
- But don't pressure you

**Why it's good:**
- Shows seriousness
- You can move faster
- Removes uncertainty

---

### **Green Flag #10: Other Founders Love Them**

**What it looks like:**
- Former founders speak highly
- Portfolio companies want to do more deals with them
- References are enthusiastic (not just "fine")
- They introduce portfolio companies to each other

**Why it's good:**
- Proven track record with founders
- Creates network value
- You'll actually benefit

## 📋 THE INVESTOR INTERVIEW (Questions to Ask)

**Before you take their money, interview THEM:**

### **Question 1: "How do you define success?"**

**Why:** Understand if their version matches yours

**Listen for:**
- ✅ "Your success is our success" = Good
- ✅ "Building a sustainable, profitable business" = Good
- ❌ "10x return in 3 years" = Pressure incoming
- ❌ "Dominate the market" = Growth-at-all-costs

---

### **Question 2: "How involved do you like to be?"**

**Why:** Gauge if they'll micromanage

**Listen for:**
- ✅ "Quarterly board meetings, available if you need me" = Good
- ✅ "I'm here if you want my input, but you're driving" = Good
- ❌ "I need weekly updates and daily dashboards" = Red flag
- ❌ "I like to be very hands-on" = Red flag

---

### **Question 3: "What happens if we miss our numbers?"**

**Why:** See if they panic or support you

**Listen for:**
- ✅ "Let's understand why and adjust" = Good
- ✅ "Setbacks are normal. How can I help?" = Good
- ❌ "We have contingency plans" = Threatening tone
- ❌ "I'll bring in new management" = They'll fire you

---

### **Question 4: "Have you ever asked a founder to pivot?"**

**Why:** See how they handle founder autonomy

**Listen for:**
- ✅ "Only if they asked for my input and agreed" = Good
- ✅ "Never. The founder knows their market" = Good
- ❌ "Yes, several times. They needed to" = Red flag
- ❌ "I do it if the data supports it" = They'll override you

---

### **Question 5: "Can you introduce me to 3 founders you've backed?"**

**Why:** Check their references

**Listen for:**
- ✅ They eagerly provide names = Confident in relationships
- ❌ They hesitate or provide only 1 = Red flag
- ❌ "I prefer not to share contacts" = Not confident

**Then call those founders:**
- "How was it working with [Investor]?"
- "Would you take money from them again?"
- "Any red flags I should know about?"

---

### **Question 6: "What's your biggest strength as an investor?"**

**Why:** See what they actually value

**Listen for:**
- ✅ Specific skills/network = Shows self-awareness
- ✅ References to helping founders = Founder-focused
- ❌ "My reputation" = Ego-driven
- ❌ "My capital" = Transactional

---

### **Question 7: "How do you handle disagreements with founders?"**

**Why:** See if they're collaborative or dictatorial

**Listen for:**
- ✅ "We talk it through and decide together" = Good
- ✅ "Founder has final say on their company" = Good
- ❌ "I explain why I'm right" = Arrogant
- ❌ "I use my leverage" = Manipulative

## 💪 HOW TO SAY NO (Sample Scripts)

### **Script #1: Not Right Fit**

"I really appreciate the offer. After thinking about it, I don't think this is the right fit for either of us. I'm looking for [X], and I don't think that's your approach. I wish you the best, and I hope we can stay in touch."

---

### **Script #2: Terms Not Working**

"I love the mission alignment, but the terms aren't workable for me. At [X equity], I can't stay motivated. I need [Y] to move forward. If you can't go there, I'll have to pass."

---

### **Script #3: Better Offer**

"I got another offer that's a better fit. I wanted to give you first right of refusal at those terms. Can you match?"

[If they can: Great]
[If they can't: "Thanks for considering. Good luck."]

---

### **Script #4: Gut Feeling**

"I trust my gut, and something feels off about this partnership. I don't think it's a good match. I'm going to pass, but I appreciate the time."

[Don't over-explain. Trust your instinct.]

---

### **Script #5: Need to Talk to Advisors**

"I want to discuss this with my advisor before committing. I'll get back to you by [date]."

[Talk to your lawyer/mentor. If they say walk, walk.]

---

## 🎁 BONUS: THE "No, But..." Response

**Sometimes you want to stay in touch but not take the deal:**

"I'm going to pass on this round, but I'd love to stay in touch. Can we grab coffee in 6 months when we have more traction?"

**Why this works:**
- Leaves door open
- Doesn't burn bridges
- Shows confidence
- Gives you leverage later
