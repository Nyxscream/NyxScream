# Red Flags & Deal Breakers - NyxScream Investor Vetting

**How to Identify Bad Fit Investors (And Walk Away)**

---

## CRITICAL MINDSET

**Before meeting any investor, remember:**

You don't need their money. You've proven the business works.

You need the RIGHT investor. Someone who gets mission. Someone who won't betray creators.

**If they show red flags, walk away. There will be better investors.**

This is non-negotiable.

---

## RED FLAG CATEGORY 1: EXIT PRESSURE

### Red Flag 1a: "What's your exit strategy?"

**Why it's a red flag:**
- Shows they're thinking flip, not long-term
- They want ROI from acquisition, not dividends
- They'll pressure for sale eventually
- Misaligned with our mission

**What to listen for:**
- "We usually target 5-7 year exit"
- "IPO is important for liquidity"
- "Strategic acquisition is natural end"
- "Exit timeline should be in business plan"

**Response:**
"We don't have an exit strategy. This company is forever. Returns come from profits, not sales. If that doesn't work for you, let's end this conversation."

**Deal Breaker Level:** HIGH

### Red Flag 1b: "We'll install a professional CEO when you're ready to scale"

**Why it's a red flag:**
- Shows they don't trust founder
- They'll replace you eventually
- Mission won't be protected
- You'll lose control

**What to listen for:**
- "Most founders step aside by Series A"
- "You're great at product, but we need business expertise"
- "Professional CEO can accelerate growth"
- "Many of our founders transition to advisor role"

**Response:**
"I stay CEO forever. Non-negotiable. If that's a problem, we're not a fit."

**Deal Breaker Level:** CRITICAL

### Red Flag 1c: "Let's aim for profitability, then explore acquisition opportunities"

**Why it's a red flag:**
- They're already thinking about selling
- They want to de-risk, then flip
- Mission is at risk post-profitability
- You'll face pressure when they see big acquisition offer

**What to listen for:**
- "Once we hit $50M+ revenue, strategic buyers will line up"
- "We'll have optionality at that point"
- "No pressure to exit, but we should have options"

**Response:**
"We will never sell, regardless of acquisition offers. Period. This is not negotiable."

**Deal Breaker Level:** CRITICAL

## RED FLAG CATEGORY 2: CONTROL PRESSURE

### Red Flag 2a: "We need board seats and veto rights"

**Why it's a red flag:**
- They want control, not advisory
- They can override founder decisions
- Mission decisions will be questioned
- You lose autonomy

**What to listen for:**
- "We need 2 board seats minimum"
- "We'll have veto on major decisions"
- "We need approval rights on hires/budget"
- "Standard governance requires investor control"

**Response:**
"You get 1 advisory board seat. No veto rights. I make final decisions on mission. If you need control, you're not the right investor."

**Deal Breaker Level:** HIGH

### Red Flag 2b: "We need to approve all major hires"

**Why it's a red flag:**
- They want operational control
- They can force out team members
- They can install their people
- Founder autonomy is gone

**What to listen for:**
- "We need sign-off on C-level hires"
- "We should meet all key people first"
- "We need veto on major hiring decisions"

**Response:**
"I hire the team I trust. You can meet them, but I decide who works here."

**Deal Breaker Level:** HIGH

### Red Flag 2c: "We need monthly board meetings"

**Why it's a red flag:**
- Too much oversight/control
- Distraction from building
- Signals lack of trust in founder
- Sets up for control escalation

**What to listen for:**
- "We need monthly detailed reporting"
- "Board meetings every 2 weeks minimum"
- "We'll have metrics tied to my approval"

**Response:**
"Monthly investor updates, quarterly calls. That's it. No micromanagement."

**Deal Breaker Level:** MEDIUM-HIGH

## RED FLAG CATEGORY 5: FINANCIAL MANIPULATION

### Red Flag 5a: "You should increase valuation to 40%+ investor equity"

**Why it's a red flag:**
- Takes too much founder control
- Dilution trap
- Sets up for future control
- Founder becomes minority shareholder

**What to listen for:**
- "40% is more typical for this size"
- "You need experienced investor partner"
- "50/50 would be better alignment"

**Response:**
"33% at $6M valuation. That's the deal. If you want more, move on."

**Deal Breaker Level:** HIGH

### Red Flag 5b: "We need 3x liquidation preference"

**Why it's a red flag:**
- Takes money off the table (before founder)
- Shows greed
- Risk mitigation at founder expense
- Unfair terms

**What to listen for:**
- "3x preference is standard for risk"
- "We need downside protection"
- "1x is too risky"

**Response:**
"1x standard preference. That's it. 3x is predatory."

**Deal Breaker Level:** MEDIUM-HIGH

### Red Flag 5c: "You need to vest your shares"

**Why it's a red flag:**
- Founder already did work (solo build)
- Vesting penalizes for past execution
- Signals distrust
- Unfair to founder

**What to listen for:**
- "We need 4-year vesting with 1-year cliff"
- "You should earn your equity"

**Response:**
"I already earned this equity (built solo). But I'll do 1-year cliff, 4-year vest to show commitment. Not more."

**Deal Breaker Level:** MEDIUM

## GREEN FLAGS (What To Look For)

**Opposite of red flags = green flags.**

### Green Flag 1: "We back founders, not just ideas"

**Why it's good:**
- Respects your execution
- Trusts founder
- Won't push for replacement
- Aligned

### Green Flag 2: "Mission matters as much as ROI"

**Why it's good:**
- True alignment
- Won't betray for profit
- Long-term thinking
- Similar values

### Green Flag 3: "Creator rights are important"

**Why it's good:**
- Understands mission
- Won't push exploitation
- Values will be protected
- True believer

### Green Flag 4: "We've backed founder-led companies"

**Why it's good:**
- Proven track record
- Won't try to replace you
- Respect for founder autonomy
- Experience with your model

### Green Flag 5: "How can we support your vision?"

**Why it's good:**
- Advisory mindset
- Respectful
- Wants to help (not control)
- Aligned

### Green Flag 6: "We'd be honored to be part of this"

**Why it's good:**
- Humility
- Belief in mission
- Excited to help
- Genuine alignment

### Green Flag 7: "This reminds me of [founder-led company]"

**Why it's good:**
- They see the model working
- They have experience with it
- They're comparing to successful examples
- Understanding of vision

### Green Flag 8: "We're willing to commit long-term"

**Why it's good:**
- No exit pressure
- Patient capital
- Mission aligned
- Forever partner

## HOW TO WALK AWAY

**If investor shows red flags:**

**Step 1: Be Clear**
"This isn't going to work. We have different values."

**Step 2: Be Firm**
"I appreciate your interest, but we're not aligned."

**Step 3: Be Respectful**
"You seem like a good investor, just not for us."

**Step 4: Be Final**
"Let's end this conversation. Good luck with your other investments."

**Don't:**
- Waffle or hedge
- Say "maybe we can compromise"
- Leave door open
- Hope they'll change

**Do:**
- Be direct
- Be confident
- Walk away
- Find better investor

---

## FINAL REMINDER

**You don't need their money.**

You've built product solo. You've proven business model. You have options.

The worst decision would be taking capital from wrong investor to get capital faster.

Better to:
- Stay bootstrapped longer
- Find right investor (takes time)
- Raise capital aligned with mission

**Money is temporary. Mission is forever. Choose wisely.**
